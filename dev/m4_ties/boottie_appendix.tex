\subsection{Tied event times in the nonparametric bootstrap}
\label{app:boottie}
Resampling subjects with replacement duplicates their event times, so every
bootstrap resample contains tied event times, on average about $110$, $340$ and
$1700$ per resample at $n=300$, $1000$ and $5000$. The simulated datasets
themselves contain none. \texttt{timereg::aalen} separates tied times by adding a
small random increment to every copy after the first and then processes the copies
one at a time. The simulation M4 results in the main text use this implementation (the applied example processes tied event times simultaneously). To gauge how
much the convention matters, we refitted the same $B=200$ resamples of the same
simulated datasets with tied copies processed simultaneously, and compared the two
conventions resample by resample. The simultaneous refits use either our own
weighted-least-squares implementation of the same fit or \texttt{timereg} applied with
each duplicated subject collapsed to one row weighted by its multiplicity; wherever
both were computed they agree to within $4\times10^{-5}$. The comparison uses no true values and so does not
address coverage.

A larger planned comparison was stopped after about $5\%$ of its replicates had run;
Web Table~\ref{tab:boottie} summarises those replicates in two separately labelled
panels. Panel A uses the production replicates that are complete in every scenario of
a given sample size: all $27$ scenarios at $n=300$ and $n=1000$ and the $9$
moderate-confounding scenarios at $n=5000$, with $11$, $10$ and $14$ replicates per
scenario ($693$ scenario--replicate pairs). Adding the $24$ pairs from incompletely
run replicates changes no SE-change, median or width-change entry by more than $0.1$
percentage point, no limit shift by more than $0.003$ standard errors, and no
${>}5\%$ share by more than $1$ percentage point. Panel B is a pilot on two further
replicates of the $27$ scenarios at each of $n=300$ and $n=1000$ and one replicate of
$3$ scenarios at $n=5000$ ($111$ distinct pairs); it is too small for Monte-Carlo
standard errors.

In Panel A the \texttt{timereg} standard error was the larger in $99.7\%$ of paired
comparisons, and in at least $98.9\%$ within every row. Its average excess was larger
at later horizons at each sample size, from under $1\%$ at $\tau=1$ to $2.6$--$5.1\%$
at $\tau=5$, and was $0.8$--$1.2\%$ for $\gamma$. The changes were right-skewed:
medians lie below the means, and single scenario--replicate pairs reached $26\%$ at
$n=1000$ ($\tau=2$) and $90\%$ at $n=300$ ($\tau=5$). The resamples of the latter
pair included risk-difference estimates outside $[-1,1]$; without the $15$ pairs
in that row with such estimates or percentile limits, the row's mean is $4.3\%$.

Percentile intervals widened by similar proportions. For $\gamma$ and from $\tau=2$
on, this was mainly because the lower limit moved down, by up to $0.11$ tie-exact
standard errors on average, while the upper limit moved by at most about $0.05$; at
$\tau=1$ both limits moved by at most about $0.02$. Because the tie convention moves
the interval limits as well as the spread, these standard-error changes do not
determine how coverage would change.

As a rough guide in the units of main-text
Table~3, we applied each scenario's standard-error change, estimated from its $11$
($n=300$) or $10$ ($n=1000$) Panel~A replicates, to its published M4 SE ratio, which
averages up to $1000$ replicates. This projects $0.908$ in place of $0.919$ at
$n=300$ and $0.839$ in place of $0.847$ at $n=1000$ (Monte-Carlo standard errors
of the projected change $0.001$ and $0.002$). The projection assumes that the
completed replicates are representative of the full run; it is not a recomputation over
the full simulation, and at $n=5000$, where the subset covers $9$ of the $27$ scenarios,
none is made. The tie-exact refits were run under
R~4.5.1 with \texttt{timereg}~2.0.7; the \texttt{timereg} arm is the main simulation
run.
